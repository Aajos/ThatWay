2. What ThatWay does

ThatWay is a compass. With a friend's permission it can show you the direction and distance to where that friend is, and guide you to where they were last seen. It also guides you to places you choose. Friends are added by username, and both people agree.

3. How tracing works, and what you agree to

  • Asking. You can only ask a friend. Nothing is shared unless they say yes. You will only ever see "Accepted" or a plain notice that the user cannot be traced; we do not say why, to protect the other person.
  • Allowing. If you are asked, you decide whether to allow it, whether it is once or ongoing, and for how long (15 minutes to 24 hours, never more than was asked). You read a safety notice, tick a box, and confirm it is you with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode.
  • Ending. A permission ends at its time, when you stop it, when either of you unfriends or blocks the other, or when the tracer's plan ends, whichever is first.
  • Limits. A tracer can look at one friend at a time and can hold at most 10 permissions. A new location can be requested at most once every 2 minutes.
  • Accuracy. Location, direction and distance are estimates. GPS and the compass can be wrong, especially indoors, close together, or near metal. ThatWay tells you how old a location is and hides the arrow when it cannot point reliably. Do not rely on ThatWay where being wrong could cause harm.
